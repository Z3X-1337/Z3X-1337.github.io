Write down what the evidence actually says.

Short notes grounded in public project behavior, general defensive practice, and authorized-review principles.

These posts are educational. They do not describe third-party findings, customer data, or claims of vulnerability discovery.

01

Security headers are control surfaces, not checkbox evidence.

Presence matters, but redirects, response variants, policy directives, and browser behavior determine what a reviewer should conclude.

 Web security
04 min 02

Authentication telemetry needs context.

Repeated failures, usernames, a later success, source address, and method form a reviewable picture — not an automatic verdict.

05 min 03

IOC provenance is part of the finding.

Indicators become more useful when the raw match, normalized value, source context, and caveats stay connected.

04 min 04

Authorization is a server-side decision.

A hidden control, client-side state, or predictable URL is not evidence that an actor may perform an action or access a resource.
